# Tensioned Hangboard Cords Full-Render Design

## Goal

Restore cords to every catalog presentation of a cord-mounted hangboard while
making the loaded orientation immediately legible. Gravity is always canvas
down, represented by the normalized vector `(0, +1)`. The board, every
identity-bearing attachment detail, and the complete visible cord system are
generated together as one new render. The accepted image has real RGBA
transparency and no wall, floor, studio sweep, or baked background.

This is a product-physics correction, not a generic cord overlay. Each render
must show the exact product revision and the real routing for the depicted
usable surface. Every depicted board is below its suspension or support while
the climber loads it downward from below. Every force-bearing cord or strap
branch therefore leaves the board taut toward an overhead support. Nothing
load-bearing—cord, tail, knot, hardware, or weight—may be below the board.
An evidence-backed non-load-bearing return segment or body sling may remain
snug against the gravity-lower side only when it is part of the exact
manufacturer topology; it must not dangle, sag, or read as the load direction.
Product-specific cord inventory, knot location and type, hole passage, and
return/crossing order remain evidence-backed within that straps-up orientation.
Rotating a board to another usable surface rotates the physical board, not
gravity. Recesses remain recesses after rotation; a concave edge or pocket must
never become a convex bump.

## Physical render contract

### Gravity and tension

Every prompt and visual review starts with a manually authored load-path
contract for the exact presentation. The contract names the loaded object, the
support or load direction, each cord attachment point visible from the chosen
view, the cord's passage through holes or around the product, and which knots,
bights, or independent strands are supported by evidence.

A loaded segment is visually taut. Straight runs may bend at a documented
hole, knot, pulley-like contact, or body sling, but may not sag, float, coil,
or terminate without a supported reason. A short free tail may appear only
when first-party evidence establishes it. It remains in the upward attachment
assembly unless it is an exact-topology, non-load-bearing lower-side return;
in either case it cannot dangle, sag, or be mistaken for the loaded path. The
scene never changes gravity to make a composition convenient.

- For every board, including lifting/no-hang tools, the board hangs below its
  support while the climber applies the downward load from below. Loaded
  branches leave the board toward the top of the canvas. The attachment may
  use holes, stopper knots, loops, or a bridle only as shown by exact product
  evidence. Nothing load-bearing—cord, tail, knot, hardware, or weight—may
  appear below the board. Exact-topology non-load-bearing return segments or
  body slings may instead remain snug against its gravity-lower side.
- The Lattice MXEdge Lift sizes and Plateau Lifting Edge retain their exact
  product identity and named usable positions while being presented straps-up:
  every force-bearing strap runs taut upward toward overhead support.
  MXEdge's documented continuous loop retains its exact hole passage and
  return order; any lower-side return segment is non-load-bearing, snug, and
  never a dangling or load-direction cue.
  Frictitious NUG retains the attachment topology established independently
  for each presentation, but its visible loaded branches also resolve upward.
- The Lattice Mini Bar retains its documented exterior-body sling routing.
  Evidence-backed U-shaped bights may wrap the lower or outer body as the
  attachment interface, but their force-bearing return branches must leave the
  board upward. The snug body sling itself is non-load-bearing; nothing
  load-bearing may hang below the board. This exception does not authorize a
  generic bottom wrap on any other board.
- Metolius Rock Rings 3D and YY Penta Evo consist of independent units. Each
  unit keeps its own documented suspension and load path; generation must not
  merge them into one unsupported bridge or make one unit hang from the other.

Cord diameter, braid, color, hole entry, crossing order, knot type, and anchor
arrangement are product identity. They are copied from exact-revision
manufacturer evidence, not from the Flash Board, a catalog baseline, another
portable board, or model intuition. A mechanically believable but
unsupported topology is still a rejection.

### Usable surfaces and relief

Some products can be pulled from more than one direction or rotated to expose
a second training surface. Each presentation is an orthographic, head-on view
of its named usable surface. The board is physically reoriented while canvas
gravity remains down and the cords are newly routed under load. An inverted
presentation is therefore not a 180-degree raster rotation of the complete
upright scene, because that would also rotate gravity and put the support below
the board.

The relief of every contact is an identity constraint shared across related
presentations. A concave pocket, incut, or cylindrical recess remains concave
when the product is inverted. Highlight and shadow placement may change with
the re-rendered orientation, but it may not visually turn a cavity into a
protrusion. Matching source and alias presentations must also preserve the
same silhouette, thickness, hole placement, contact count, material, and
relative contact dimensions.

For a canonical presentation and any inverted alias, the board body is
rendered at the same scale and the same normalized canvas position, with an
equal image aspect ratio. Cords can change around it to remain physically
loaded. Candidate drift in board scale, translation, perspective, or aspect
ratio is rejected and regenerated; it is never compensated for by moving
individual hold paths.

## Evidence and generation contract

### Complete first-party input set

Before generation, freeze the complete official manufacturer photo set for
the exact physical revision. The audit records every direct URL, publisher,
review date, revision applicability, image role, original byte SHA-256,
decoded-pixel SHA-256, dimensions, color mode, and local workspace-owned cache
path. A product page or catalogue may establish a claim, but every official
manufacturer photograph gathered for the exact revision is passed as an
actual image reference to every generation call for that package. Listing a
URL in the prompt or ledger is not a substitute for supplying the pixels.

The image generator accepts at most five references. When the complete
official set is larger, compose it into at most five lossless evidence atlas
pages. Atlas construction may add neutral padding and labels outside the
panels, but it must not crop, resample, warp, rotate, recolor, retouch, or
compress a source image. Before a call, an automated check extracts every
panel and proves that its dimensions and decoded pixels round-trip exactly to
the frozen source. The ledger records atlas hashes, panel coordinates, source
mapping, and the exact ordered atlas list supplied to the call. If the full
official set cannot be represented within five verified atlas pages, the
presentation blocks rather than omitting or degrading a source.

Independent photographs can corroborate mechanics or ambiguous depth, but do
not displace the complete first-party input set. If they are supplied to the
generator, they receive the same URL, revision, role, and hash treatment.
Conflicting sources are resolved to one named physical revision before a call;
features from different revisions are never blended.

Current Hang Ten assets are not evidence. They may be supplied only as
non-authoritative composition or catalog-style references when the five-image
limit has room after the complete official evidence set. They are never edit
targets, never establish topology, and never excuse a missing official image.

### Whole-image generation

Each candidate is a new-image generation, not an edit of the existing board
and not a programmatic cord overlay. The model synthesizes the entire board,
all visible cords, knots, hardware, material cues, and surface relief together
in one output. The prompt includes the presentation's evidence-backed
load-path contract, gravity direction, usable surface, relief constraints,
exact product revision, required aspect ratio and composition, and forbidden
features. It also requests a flat chroma background with no environmental
objects or ground plane.

Generate exactly two initial candidates per unique PNG. Review both against
the complete evidence set and record concrete evidence-based dispositions. A
single targeted third candidate is allowed only after the first two have been
rejected and the third prompt addresses their named failure. If the targeted
third candidate also fails, mark the asset blocked and escalate the evidence,
prompt contract, or product classification; do not continue an unbounded
sequence of aesthetic retries.

This is the bounded policy for the original batch. A later user-directed,
quality-first correction may expressly authorize additional whole-image
retries for the affected assets. That correction does not retroactively comply
with the original cap: retain every rejected and selected candidate, prompt,
reference list, hash, and disposition in its provenance, and identify the
override and review finding that required the correction.

Every call record contains the full prompt, model/tool version when exposed,
generation time, exact ordered reference hashes, output hash, output
dimensions, and candidate disposition. A pleasing result is insufficient.
Acceptance requires exact-revision likeness, complete component inventory,
correct attachment topology, taut load path, correct relief, consistent board
scale and position, and successful transparency conversion.

### Transparency and permitted output processing

Request a documented flat chroma color, normally `#00ff00`, behind the complete
object. Convert that color to alpha with one deterministic local chroma-removal
step. Chroma removal, including the narrowly bounded edge decontamination
needed to eliminate a green fringe, is the only permitted processing of a
generated candidate.

Do not crop, resize, rotate, register, align, warp, composite, paint, clone,
sharpen, blur, color-grade, relight, replace cords, patch topology, or combine
parts from candidates. Required framing, aspect ratio, board position, and
dimensions must be obtained from generation. Atlas construction is input
preparation and does not authorize any corresponding transformation of model
output.

The promoted file is an RGBA PNG with meaningful transparency. Its minimum
alpha is zero; all four corners are transparent; no chroma-colored matte or
off-white studio background remains; and cord, knot, hardware, wood, stone,
metal, or plastic edges show no key fringe or accidental transparency. A PNG
that merely has an alpha channel while every pixel is opaque fails.

## Canonical geometry and inverted aliases

The saved canonical hold path remains the sole source for rendering,
highlighting, and hit testing. `sourcePresentationID` continues to express
that an alias shows the same physical contacts, and `isInverted: true`
continues to select a 180-degree orientation. The alias does not duplicate or
override canonical hold geometry.

Cord-inclusive transparent canvases can place the board body away from the
full image center. Rotating canonical geometry around `(0.5, 0.5)` then makes
the overlay miss an otherwise correctly positioned inverted board. Add one
optional, backward-compatible alias field:

```json
"geometryRotationAnchor": {"x": 0.5, "y": 0.68}
```

Both values are normalized image coordinates. The field is legal only on a
presentation that has `sourcePresentationID` and `isInverted: true`. When it
is omitted, the anchor defaults to `(0.5, 0.5)`, preserving every existing
package's behavior. For an anchor `(a_x, a_y)`, every normalized canonical
point `(x, y)` projects to the alias as:

```text
x' = 2a_x - x
y' = 2a_y - y
```

The same projection helper must drive the rendered `BoardHoldPathShape`,
active-highlight shape, marker center, accessibility/selection target, and hit
test in SwiftUI. Workbench alias preview and interaction use the identical
math. Transform final normalized path points consistently rather than applying
different frame and marker approximations.

Schema and package validation require finite `x` and `y` values within
`[0, 1]`, the alias-only relationship above, an equal source/target aspect
ratio, and transformed geometry that remains within the target canvas. A
source presentation may not declare an anchor. A non-inverted alias may not
declare an anchor. An inverted alias without a custom anchor remains valid and
uses the center default.

The anchor is a presentation-level correction for the board's physical pivot,
not a license to fit geometry to a drifting render. The operator chooses it by
reviewing the board body's same-scale, same-position source and alias images.
Per-hold offsets, alias geometry overrides, arbitrary affine matrices, and
raster-derived fitting are rejected. If one anchor cannot align all contacts,
the image pair is regenerated or the presentation is blocked.

After each promoted image, an operator reviews every canonical hold in
Workbench against primary evidence and the accepted render. Incorrect paths
are deliberately redrawn. Exact left/right mirroring is used only for a
physically symmetric product, and a supported shape constraint is selected
only when the operator establishes the actual circle, oval, pill, rounded
rectangle, or rectangle. The path remains authoritative.

No image-driven hold detection, segmentation, generated mask or contour,
source registration or alignment, raster vectorization, automatic path
simplification, automatic cropping, or proposal/refine/promote geometry
workflow is permitted. Generation neither proposes nor validates hold
geometry.

## Provenance audit and package boundary

Create a new machine-readable cord-render audit outside package roots, with a
human-readable companion narrative. The implementation plan will assign their
dated paths under `docs/source-audits/`. The audit has one presentation record
for all 49 presentations and a deduplicated asset record for all 48 PNGs, so
the Port-A-Board sharing is explicit rather than counted twice.

Each presentation record includes:

- package ID and directory, presentation ID and name, asset path, source alias,
  aspect ratio, physical revision, and material;
- mechanical family, gravity vector, usable surface, manually authored load
  path, attachment points, knot/bight rules, and relief identity constraints;
- every official source and optional corroborating source with its URL, role,
  date, revision claim, original hash, decoded-pixel hash, dimensions, and
  workspace cache ownership;
- verified atlas pages, panel mappings, round-trip results, page hashes, and
  exact ordered references used by every call;
- complete prompts, candidate hashes and dimensions, alpha-conversion hash,
  accepted or rejected disposition, and a concrete reason for each candidate;
- the selected asset's on-disk hash, focused alpha-test result, package
  validation result, geometry review, alias-anchor review where applicable,
  and independent reviewer decision; and
- the actual iOS screenshot path and hash showing this exact presentation with
  one real hold selected and highlighted.

The existing 2026-08-30 presentation-remediation manifest is an immutable
record of the catalog state it audited. Its historical hashes are expected to
stop matching when this work replaces assets. Do not edit, rebaseline, or
retroactively mark that manifest green. The new audit and companion narrative
explicitly supersede its background requirement and explain the new asset
lineage.

This design also supersedes the stale cord-only pixel-preservation approach.
The previous idea of preserving every board pixel while adding or repairing
only a cord layer is not the target. Likewise, the former off-white-background
remediation rule no longer applies to this cohort. The accepted artifact is a
new complete board-and-cord render on real transparency, governed by the
evidence and geometry controls in this document.

Each package root continues to contain only `board.json` and `assets/`. Files
below `assets/` must exactly equal the paths declared by the package. Evidence
downloads, atlases, prompts, rejected candidates, conversion intermediates,
review notes, scripts, and screenshots do not enter a package directory.

## Parallel ownership, review, and promotion

Generation can run in parallel by product because packages do not share
candidate state. Assign one product owner to one package at a time. Each owner
writes only to a workspace-owned directory named with the worktree owner, for
example `.context/joyful-donkey-cords-aelith-cyclops-011-run-1`, and records
ownership immediately. Owners may not write candidates directly into
`Hangboards/`.

Each owner freezes evidence, verifies atlases, writes the mechanical contract,
runs the original bounded candidate sequence (or a separately recorded
user-directed correction override), performs chroma removal, runs focused
alpha checks, and submits an asset packet with hashes and dispositions. A
different reviewer compares the selected candidate with all official inputs
and the mechanical contract. Only a designated integration owner may promote
an independently accepted asset, update `board.json`, update the new audit,
and run repository gates. Source/alias pairs are promoted atomically so an
upright image cannot ship without its reviewed inverted companion.

All supported `cord_render_assets` writers for one owner context are
serialized by a process-local reentrant lock and a cross-process `flock`, both
held through publication commit and cleanup. Ordinary external changes at
observable transaction boundaries are still detected and preserved. Arbitrary
same-UID direct mutation during an exact name-based `unlink` or `rmdir` is
outside the supported concurrency contract because macOS/POSIX provides no
inode-conditional delete primitive. Created output-parent directories are
monotonic and are never destructively rolled back. Publication performs one
all-output final precommit verification before entering an explicit
`COMMITTED` state: a precommit error rolls back, while a postcommit cleanup
error never rolls back, leaves every output consistently committed, and raises
with `publication committed; cleanup state is unproven`. Every generated old-
or new-output quarantine name is registered before its syscall and is later
reconciled or disclosed.

Every external or generated resource uses the owner name `joyful-donkey`.
Before creating a simulator, image-generation job with a deletable handle, or
other external resource, the owning worker records its exact identifier and
installs an exit/archive trap that shuts down and deletes only those exact
owned resources. Cleanup verifies absence before the worker reports
completion. Shared, standard, and unknown resources are never deleted.
Temporary source caches, atlases, rejected candidates, chroma intermediates,
Derived Data, and simulator captures remain under owned `.context` paths until
their hashes and required review artifacts have been recorded, then are
removed by the owner. Final user-facing iOS captures are promoted to a dated
path below `docs/pr-screenshots/`, outside every package root, before temporary
capture directories are cleaned.

## Validation and screenshot contract

### Automated gates

Add focused tests for the optional alias anchor in the Python package schema,
Swift package decoding/encoding, SwiftUI projection helper, and Workbench
preview. Tests cover default-center compatibility, a non-center anchor, invalid
coordinates, illegal source/non-inverted placement, out-of-canvas projection,
path/highlight/marker/hit-test agreement, and writer round-trip behavior.

Add a focused transparency gate for every modified PNG. It requires PNG/RGBA,
minimum alpha zero, transparent corners, no opaque background flood, no
detectable chroma fringe, and declared dimensions/aspect ratio. This is
separate from generic package validation because a syntactically valid opaque
PNG is not acceptable for this cohort.

Run the current package and source-boundary checks after each reviewable batch
and at final inventory:

```sh
rtk scripts/hangboard-packages.sh validate --root Hangboards --final-inventory
rtk scripts/hangboard-packages.sh status --root Hangboards
rtk uv run --with pytest --with Pillow --with PyYAML python -m pytest -q Tools/HangboardPackages/tests
rtk uv run --with pytest python -m pytest -q Tools/HangboardWorkbench/tests
rtk scripts/verify-board-source-boundary-manifest.sh
rtk xcodebuild build-for-testing -project HangTen.xcodeproj -scheme HangTen -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator'
```

The bounded simulator test pass also runs
`BoardPackageStoreTests`, `BoardPackageWriterTests`, and
`BoardSourceBoundaryTests`, including new anchor cases. A passing generic
validator cannot waive an evidence, alpha, mechanics, geometry, or visual
failure.

### Manual geometry and iOS proof

For each of the 49 presentation records, inspect normal rendering, all-active
rendering, each logical hold and every piece of a multi-piece hold, selected
highlight, marker center, and hit-test alignment in Workbench and the app.
Related source and inverted aliases are compared side by side to confirm that
the board body stayed at the same scale and position and that concavity,
silhouette, and contact identity survived the rotation.

Capture at least one actual app screenshot for every presentation after final
assets and geometry are installed. Each of the 49 required screenshots visibly
shows one real hold selected and highlighted in the exact named presentation;
a generator preview, composited mockup, all-active-only frame, or screenshot of
a different alias does not count. Use the isolated iOS validation workflow,
an explicitly owned simulator UUID, workspace-specific Derived Data, and an
exact presentation-selection route. The new audit maps every capture path and
hash to its package and presentation. Multi-presentation packages require one
distinct capture per record even when two records currently share a PNG.

## Rollout and failure handling

Roll out in mechanically coherent, reviewable batches rather than one catalog
replacement. Land the backward-compatible alias-anchor model and tests first.
Then complete evidence and render packets by family: ordinary overhead
suspension; straps-up lifting/no-hang tools; exterior sling; and
independent-unit suspension. Every family keeps the board below overhead
support. Within a batch, promote only independently reviewed packages and
rerun the complete package inventory so an undeclared or half-written package
cannot enter the app.

Reject a candidate when any of the following is true:

- an official photo was omitted from the actual call or an atlas panel does
  not round-trip pixel-for-pixel;
- the product revision, silhouette, material, dimensions, holes, contacts,
  cord inventory, knot, attachment, crossing order, or usable surface differs
  from the evidence;
- gravity does not point canvas down, a loaded segment sags, a force-bearing
  branch points the wrong way, anything load-bearing appears below the board,
  or an unsupported/dangling lower-side wrap replaces real routing;
- a concave contact appears convex, a convex feature becomes a recess, or a
  rotated view loses the source surface's physical identity;
- source and alias board bodies differ in scale, position, aspect ratio, or
  perspective, or require per-hold geometry compensation;
- the output includes a baked background, chroma fringe, environment, crop,
  missing component, or any post-processing beyond chroma removal; or
- canonical paths, selected highlights, markers, accessibility targets, or
  hit tests do not align in Workbench and the app.

Block a presentation, record the exact missing evidence or failed gate, and
continue independent packages when source evidence cannot establish an exact
routing; the complete official image set is unavailable or cannot fit in five
lossless atlases; all three original-batch candidates fail without a later
user-directed correction override; a same-scale alias pair cannot be
generated; or one rotation anchor cannot align the complete contact set. Do
not invent missing mechanics to make the batch appear complete. The
Port-A-Board option-4 record begins in this evidence-blocked state and leaves
it only when first-party routing evidence is recorded.

A batch is never declared complete with an unreported block. A blocked record
may remain out of a promoted source/alias pair while unrelated complete
packages proceed, but final cohort acceptance requires every one of the 49
records to be either successfully accepted or deliberately removed from scope
by a separately approved, evidence-backed product decision.

## Acceptance criteria

The change is complete only when all of the following are true:

- The new audit accounts for exactly 20 packages, 49 presentation records, and
  48 unique PNG paths, including all four Flash presentations and the explicit
  Port-A-Board shared-path relationship.
- Every accepted unique PNG is a whole-image, from-scratch board-and-cord
  generation whose call actually received the complete verified official
  manufacturer photo set for the exact revision.
- Every visible loaded cord follows the presentation's evidence-backed
  topology and canvas-down gravity contract: the board remains below support,
  the climber loads downward from below, and every force-bearing branch leaves
  upward, including lifting-tool, Mini-Bar sling, and independent-unit routing.
  Any lower-side return segment or body sling is documented, non-load-bearing,
  snug, and unable to read as the load direction.
- All promoted assets are genuine RGBA transparency with clear corners and no
  chroma or off-white fringe, and no output transform other than documented
  chroma removal occurred.
- All related usable-surface views preserve exact product identity and surface
  relief; concave contacts remain concave; source and alias board bodies stay
  at equal scale, position, and aspect ratio.
- The optional `geometryRotationAnchor` is backward compatible, schema-valid,
  shared by rendering and interaction code, and used only where a reviewed
  cord-inclusive alias canvas needs a non-center physical pivot.
- Every changed hold path was manually reviewed and, when necessary, directly
  redrawn; no prohibited image-derived geometry method was used.
- Focused alpha and alias tests, complete Python package and Workbench suites,
  Swift package/source-boundary suites, package validation/status, source
  boundary verification, and the bounded iOS build all pass.
- There is one final selected-hold highlighted app screenshot for each of the
  49 presentations, with audit-matched paths and hashes.
- The historical remediation manifest remains byte-for-byte untouched, the
  new narrative states what it supersedes, package directories contain only
  `board.json` plus declared assets, and all temporary or external resources
  owned by `joyful-donkey` have been verified deleted.
