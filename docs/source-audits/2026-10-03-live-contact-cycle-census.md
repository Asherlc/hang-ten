ID, and 346 lose then regain an active ID two corrections later. **That alone
does not establish contact discovery failure.** Re-querying each of the 2,457
missing active triangle features at the exact next pre-correction geometry finds

The merit penalty is **exactly one on every correction**. Its decrease therefore
cannot explain these cycles. Portal material segments change in 179 transitions;
changing gradients occur in 1,114. Those counts include ordinary motion and do
not prove either mechanism causes the small-correction tail.

Step 109 is a reproducible 11-QP example: after the first 160.05 µm correction,
full corrections repeat at 4.36–4.86 µm. Its merit genuinely decreases by roughly
1–6 nanometres of its length-dominated penalty; this is far above the 1e-18 slack.
A half step is followed by a 0.265 µm full correction. Retaining duplicate face
IDs and changing a constant penalty are closed as unsupported fixes. A stricter
global sufficient-decrease test is a distinct possible discriminator; it preserves
the merit function and can only reject trials the existing rule would accept.
It is not evidence of real-time performance.
