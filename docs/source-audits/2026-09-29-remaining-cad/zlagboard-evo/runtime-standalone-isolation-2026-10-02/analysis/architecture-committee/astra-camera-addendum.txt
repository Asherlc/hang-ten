**On-demand hand rendering is the preferable renderer-replacement fallback**

Your distinction is correct: rendering hands to images avoids the unresolved RealityRenderer collision-picking requirement. The live board can retain its existing RealityView, contact collisions, targeted gestures, projection, and accessibility implementation.

That makes it a substantially narrower candidate than replacing the board renderer:

- Retain the existing hand geometry, pose logic, selected-finger coloring, orthographic camera, and lighting.
- Render when pose, fingers, viewport, orbit, or zoom changes; coalesce gesture updates rather than add a perpetual redraw loop.
- Present only completed output for the current revision, rejecting late frames from older requests.
- Preserve hand exploration controls, labels, and interaction through those event-driven renders.

Two gates remain. First, a correct offscreen image must actually replace the displayed image. Second, RealityRenderer still shares RealityKit’s backend: removing a persistent second onscreen host does not prove that concurrent offscreen hand rendering is harmless. The feasibility test must exercise hand updates while the board changes red → blue → red, and verify that board interaction remains live.

Inspector presentation also needs explicit coverage; opening a separate hand RealityView over a retained workout could recreate the problematic arrangement.
